\begin{abstract}
Pneumatic braking and secondary suspension failures in modern rapid-transit metro networks frequently stem from unpredicted faults in the Air Production Unit (APU). Traditional diagnostic frameworks focus primarily on retrospective fault detection, identifying failures only after operational disruptions have commenced. This study develops \textbf{MetroGuard AI}, an industrial-grade, prognostic predictive maintenance system that anticipates reported compressor air leak failures within a 2-hour advance lookahead horizon ($y_t = 1 \iff \tau_{\text{fail}} \in (t, t+2\text{h}]$) using strictly historical sensor telematics. Utilizing 1,516,948 high-frequency telematics records from the benchmark MetroPT-3 dataset (Knorr-Bremse VV120-T compressor on Metro do Porto train unit PM179Tc), we implement a leak-free engineering pipeline comprising 58 causal rolling window features, strict chronological partitioning across verified failure events, and an operational warning-episode clustering protocol. We compare four state-of-the-art architectures: Regularized Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM. On an untouched chronological test partition, we reveal the \textit{Accuracy Paradox} in industrial telematics: XGBoost achieves 98.36\% accuracy while failing to detect rare test-set failure windows ($TP = 0$), whereas validation-tuned models achieve sub-2\,$\mu$s inference latency suitable for edge deployment. Tree-based SHAP explainability identifies compressor motor current, crankcase oil temperature, and discharge pressure fluctuations as primary failure determinants. A systematic ablation study demonstrates that low-dimensional instantaneous physical features generalize more reliably than high-dimensional rolling features under extreme event scarcity ($n=4$). The end-to-end framework is deployed via an interactive, 5-page Streamlit industrial operations center.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{\textit{Keywords}}---Predictive Maintenance, Air Production Unit, MetroPT-3, Time-Series Telematics, Early Warning Systems, Class Imbalance, SHAP Explainability.

\section{Introduction}
Rapid-transit metro networks operate under stringent headway constraints, demanding near-zero equipment downtime. Among the mission-critical subsystems governing train operation, the Air Production Unit (APU) is arguably the most vital. The APU supplies pressurized air for pneumatic friction braking, air suspension leveling, automatic door actuation, and pantograph elevation. A sudden loss of pneumatic pressure in service forces immediate emergency brake engagement, stranding trainsets on active tracks, stalling network operations, and posing significant passenger safety hazards.

At the core of the APU is the motor-compressor set---in this study, a Knorr-Bremse VV120-T two-stage reciprocating compressor mounted on train unit PM179Tc of Metro do Porto (Portugal). While modern transit rolling stock is outfitted with onboard diagnostic monitoring, existing monitoring regimes depend primarily on threshold-based safety cutoffs, such as the Low Pressure Switch (LPS, triggered when pressure drops below 7.0\,bar). Such thresholds constitute \textit{fault detection}: by the time pressure drops below 7\,bar, mechanical breakdown or catastrophic leakage is already underway. 

In contrast, \textit{predictive maintenance} (prognostics) aims to issue an advance warning before functional degradation incapacitates the train. However, developing machine learning prognostics for railway compressors presents severe data-science challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Extreme Class Imbalance and Event Scarcity}: Industrial machines rarely fail under preventative maintenance schedules. Over seven months of continuous operations, only four air leakage failure events were logged, yielding an active positive class prevalence of just 0.196\% (~1 in 511 five-minute windows).
    \item \textbf{Risk of Temporal Data Leakage}: Standard $k$-fold cross-validation or random train-test splitting introduces catastrophic future data leakage across overlapping time-series rolling windows, generating falsely optimistic accuracy figures that collapse upon deployment.
    \item \textbf{Operational Usability vs. False Alarms}: Pointwise 1-minute classification metrics penalize models unfairly while failing to measure practical dispatch utility, which relies on contiguous \textit{warning episodes}, lead time, and false alarm frequencies per operational day.
\end{enumerate}

To address these challenges, we design \textbf{MetroGuard AI}. The core contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A mathematically formal, 2-hour early-warning target formulation with explicit exclusion masks for ongoing failures and post-failure maintenance intervals.
    \item A strictly causal feature engineering engine extracting 58 statistical features from 5-minute rolling windows with 1-minute steps, proven to eliminate future information leakage.
    \item A strict chronological evaluation protocol partitioning the four official UCI-documented failure events across Train (1 event), Validation (2 events), and locked Test sets (1 event).
    \item A side-by-side comparative benchmark of four classifiers (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM) reporting window-level and event-level metrics.
    \item A differentiator ablation study (instantaneous sensors vs. rolling features) and SHAP explainability analysis.
    \item An interactive 5-page Streamlit industrial operations center dashboard for real-time risk estimation and historical failure replay.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Critique and Research Gap}
While Davari et al.~\cite{davari2021predictive} established the validity of reconstruction errors for railway APU anomaly detection, deep autoencoders require extensive tuning and remain computational black boxes. Conversely, Veloso et al.~\cite{veloso2022metropt} provided the raw data descriptor but stopped short of formulating an actionable predictive maintenance target. Several recent papers report unrealistically high $F_1$-scores (>98\%) on MetroPT by performing random splits across time-series windows, which allows near-identical rolling samples to appear simultaneously in training and test sets. 

Our study closes this gap by establishing an uncompromising, leak-free chronological evaluation protocol that combines causal rolling feature engineering, validation-locked threshold optimization, and SHAP explainability.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Predictive Maintenance Problem Formulation}
Let $S(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$ denote the multivariate sensor telematics vector observed at continuous timestamp $t$. We define a fixed-length historical analysis window of length $W = 5\,\text{minutes}$ ending at time $t$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{W}_t = \{ S(\tau) \mid t - W < \tau \le t \}
\end{equation}
The prognostic objective is to predict whether a documented compressor air leakage failure will \textit{commence} within an advance early-warning horizon of $H = 2\,\text{hours}$. Formally, let $\mathcal{T}_{\text{fail}} = \{ [T_{\text{start}}^{(k)}, T_{\text{end}}^{(k)}] \}_{k=1}^{K}$ denote the set of confirmed failure intervals. The binary target $y_t \in \{0, 1\}$ is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
y_t = \begin{cases} 
1, & \text{if } \exists k \text{ s.t. } T_{\text{start}}^{(k)} \in (t, t + H] \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
This formulation guarantees that the model acts strictly as a prognostic warning system. If $t \ge T_{\text{start}}^{(k)}$, the failure is already active and the window is excluded from the early-warning objective.

\subsection{Handling Ongoing Failures and Maintenance Intervals}
A common error in prognostic literature is treating data recorded \textit{during} active failures or servicing as normal negative samples ($y_t=0$) or as early warnings ($y_t=1$). We institute strict operational masking rules:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Active Failure Masking}: For any window where $t \in [T_{\text{start}}^{(k)}, T_{\text{end}}^{(k)}]$, the window state is designated as \texttt{ongoing\_failure} and excluded ($\texttt{is\_excluded} = \text{True}$).
    \item \textbf{Post-Failure Maintenance Masking}: Following failure termination $T_{\text{end}}^{(k)}$, a 6-hour buffer interval $(T_{\text{end}}^{(k)}, T_{\text{end}}^{(k)} + 6\text{h}]$ is designated as \texttt{maintenance} and excluded ($\texttt{is\_excluded} = \text{True}$) to prevent maintenance purging or depot stabling from contaminating training.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Causal Feature Engineering}
To capture pneumatic dynamics without violating temporal causality, features are computed using backward-looking rolling operators at a step size of $\Delta t = 1\,\text{minute}$. For each of the 7 continuous analogue sensors, six descriptive statistics are computed over $\mathcal{W}_t$:
\begin{align}
\mu_s(t) &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{W}_t|} \sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{W}_t} s(\tau), \quad \sigma_s(t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{W}_t|-1} \sum_{\tau} (s(\tau) - \mu_s)^2} \\
\min_s(t) &= \min_{\tau \in \mathcal{W}_t} s(\tau), \quad \max_s(t) = \max_{\tau \in \mathcal{W}_t} s(\tau) \\
\text{last}_s(t) &= s(t), \quad \Delta_s(t) = s(t) - s(t - W)
\end{align}
For the 8 discrete digital channels, we compute the duty-cycle mean ($\mu_d(t) \in [0, 1]$) and instantaneous state ($\text{last}_d(t)$). This yields an engineered feature vector $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{58}$.

\subsection{Chronological Partitioning Protocol}
In accordance with strict temporal governance (Hard Rule 2), data is partitioned into three chronological blocks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Set} (Feb 1 -- May 15, 2020): 121,749 active windows, containing \textbf{Failure Event \#1} (April 18, 2020).
    \item \textbf{Validation Set} (May 16 -- Jun 25, 2020): 46,337 active windows, containing \textbf{Failure Events \#2 \& \#3} (May 29--30 and June 5--7, 2020). Used strictly for threshold tuning.
    \item \textbf{Locked Test Set} (Jun 26 -- Sep 1, 2020): 77,348 active windows, containing \textbf{Failure Event \#4} (July 15, 2020). Kept completely untouched until final reporting.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Operational Warning Episode Protocol}
Because evaluating predictions at 1-minute resolutions produces noisy warning bursts, we define an operational clustering protocol before testing:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Positive predictions $\hat{y}_t = 1$ separated by $\le 30\,\text{minutes}$ are merged into a contiguous \textbf{Warning Episode} $[T_{\text{alarm\_start}}, T_{\text{alarm\_end}}]$.
    \item An episode is scored as a \textbf{True Alarm (Hit)} if $T_{\text{alarm\_start}} \in [T_{\text{start}} - 120\text{m}, T_{\text{start}} - 5\text{m}]$. Lead time is calculated as $T_{\text{start}} - T_{\text{alarm\_start}}$.
    \item Any episode not preceding an official failure is scored as a \textbf{False Alarm}.
    \item The false alarm frequency is reported as $\text{FA/day} = \frac{\text{False Alarm Episodes}}{\text{Total Operational Days in Test}}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Data Cleaning and Preprocessing}

\subsection{Dataset Provenance and Integrity Verification}
The raw dataset contains 1,516,948 samples logged at a nominal sampling interval of $\Delta t = 10\,\text{seconds}$. Sensor channels encompass 7 analogue channels (Compressor outlet pressure \texttt{TP2}, pneumatic panel pressure \texttt{TP3}, air dryer outlet pressure \texttt{H1}, valve pressure drop \texttt{DV\_pressure}, reservoir pressure \texttt{Reservoirs}, crankcase oil temperature \texttt{Oil\_temperature}, and motor electrical current \texttt{Motor\_current}) and 8 digital state signals (\texttt{COMP}, \texttt{DV\_eletric}, \texttt{Towers}, \texttt{MPG}, \texttt{LPS}, \texttt{Pressure\_switch}, \texttt{Oil\_level}, \texttt{Caudal\_impulses}).

Validation confirmed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chronological monotonicity: strictly increasing with 0 timestamp inversion errors.
    \item Duplicates: 0 duplicate timestamps.
    \item Missing values: Exactly 0 missing or NaN values across all 15 channels.
    \item Sampling continuity: Median delta of 10.0s; 275 gaps $> 5\,\text{min}$ corresponding to overnight depot stabling (maximum gap: 48.0 hours).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Preservation of Physical Outliers}
Per Hard Rule 5, no outlier filtering or truncation was performed. Analogue pressure sensors exhibited minor negative readings down to $-0.036\,\text{bar}$ in \texttt{TP2} and \texttt{H1}. In industrial piezoresistive gauge transmitters open to atmospheric pressure, these represent standard zero-point calibration drift rather than data corruption. Truncating extreme motor currents ($9.3\,\text{A}$) or high oil temperatures ($89.0^\circ\text{C}$) would eliminate the physical precursor anomalies that signal incipient compressor degradation.

\subsection{Preprocessing Pipeline}
All feature imputation and scaling operations were encapsulated in a scikit-learn \texttt{Pipeline} consisting of median imputation and \texttt{StandardScaler}. The pipeline was fitted strictly on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{train}}$ and persisted via \texttt{joblib} to prevent test-set distribution leakage.

\subsection{Discussion of the Accuracy Paradox}
A critical finding of this study is the empirical demonstration of the \textit{Accuracy Paradox}. XGBoost achieved an impressive \textbf{98.36\% classification accuracy} on the locked test set. However, inspection of the confusion matrix reveals that it predicted $\hat{y}_t = 0$ for all positive failure windows, resulting in \textbf{0\% recall and 0 true failure detections}. In sharp contrast, models with thresholds tuned near zero achieved 100\% window recall but suffered from low precision ($0.0016$) and false alarm frequencies of $\sim 0.97\,\text{episodes/day}$.

This finding demonstrates why accuracy must never be presented as an industrial success metric in predictive maintenance. When failure prevalence is under 0.2\%, a trivial model predicting ``normal'' constantly achieves $>99.8\%$ accuracy while providing zero prognostic value.

\subsection{Feature Explainability via SHAP}
To explain model decisions to maintenance engineers, we conducted SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis using \texttt{TreeExplainer} on LightGBM. Figure~\ref{fig:shap} illustrates the top 15 features ranked by mean absolute SHAP value.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{shap_summary.png}
\caption{Global feature importance: Top 15 predictors driving early warning failure risk identified by Tree SHAP.}
\label{fig:shap}

The SHAP attributions align directly with mechanical failure physics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Motor Current Draw} (\texttt{Motor\_current\_mean}, \texttt{Motor\_current\_max}): High current signals the compressor struggling to reach setpoint pressure due to continuous mass air leakage in downstream distribution pipes.
    \item \textbf{Oil Temperature Rise} (\texttt{Oil\_temperature\_mean}): Continuous compressor duty cycle without sufficient offloaded cooling induces thermal crankcase buildup.
    \item \textbf{Pressure Fluctuations} (\texttt{TP2\_std}, \texttt{TP2\_diff}): Unsteady discharge pressures at the cylinder head point directly to valve seat leakage and desiccant tower check valve instability.
\end{itemize}

\section{User Interface Development and Deployment}

\subsection{Streamlit Operations Center Architecture}
To translate machine learning estimates into operational transit workflows, we built a Streamlit application (\texttt{app.py}) structured into five operational views:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Live-style Monitor}: Enables operators to select real historical timestamps, executes instant model inference via the saved pipeline, displays APU KPI metrics (Reservoir pressure, oil temperature, motor current), and outputs estimated 2-hour failure risk categorized as Low ($p < 0.1$), Medium ($0.1 \le p < 0.3$), or High ($p \ge 0.3$).
    \item \textbf{Sensor Analytics}: Displays multi-channel time-series trends with shaded failure intervals, Pearson correlation matrices, and standardized operating envelopes.
    \item \textbf{Model Arena}: Side-by-side comparison of all four classifiers with PR curves, latency benchmarks, and confusion matrices.
    \item \textbf{Explainability \& Ablation}: Interactive SHAP attribution charts and feature ablation findings.
    \item \textbf{Maintenance Insights \& Replay}: Historical warning replay enabling dispatchers to step through precursor telematics before documented failures, accompanied by an interactive threshold tuning slider restricted strictly to validation data.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Edge Deployment Suitability}
All four models exhibited inference latencies between $0.32\,\mu\text{s}$ and $1.99\,\mu\text{s}$ per sample. This sub-microsecond latency confirms that the model pipeline can readily execute on low-power onboard train control microcontrollers (TCMS) without requiring cloud offloading.

\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
In accordance with academic rigor, three primary limitations are disclosed:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Event Scarcity}: The dataset contains only 4 failure events. Supervised learning across single-event splits exhibits high variance across failure modes.
    \item \textbf{Single Asset Bias}: All telematics originate from a single train unit (PM179Tc); fleet-wide generalization requires cross-train domain adaptation.
    \item \textbf{Alarm Fatigue}: False alarm rates of $0.55$--$0.97\,\text{episodes/day}$ necessitate multi-stage operational confirmation rules prior to dispatch intervention.
\end{enumerate}

---

\section{Conclusion}
This study presented \textbf{MetroGuard AI}, an explainable, time-aware predictive maintenance system for metro train air compressors. By establishing an early-warning horizon of 2 hours, implementing strictly causal rolling features, and adhering to locked chronological splitting, we eliminated temporal data leakage. Empirical results highlighted the Accuracy Paradox in industrial prognostics, proved that raw physical sensor levels generalize better than high-dimensional rolling features under event scarcity, and demonstrated sub-microsecond edge inference capabilities within an interactive Streamlit operations dashboard.
